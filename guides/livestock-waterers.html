Livestock waterers: capacity, freeze, and refill

Agrivia editorial · August 29, 2026 · Educational overview

Water space and refill rate fail before “the tank looks full” fails. This page covers categories, not a ranked list of brands Agrivia installed.

Open tanks vs automatic waterers

Open tanks are cheap to understand and easy to clean if you can dump them. They heat in the sun and freeze at the surface. Automatic energy-free or electric units keep a smaller surface open and refill as animals drink. They need a reliable supply line and a plan for a stuck float or a chewed hose.

Capacity and access

Peak use is after feed and in the afternoon heat. Count linear inches of drinkable edge, not only gallons. Dominant animals will block a single small bowl. Extension publications often publish inches-per-head starting points — use your class of stock and then watch the pen.

Freeze and power

Electric heaters, buried lines below frost, and energy-free designs all appear in northern lots. Ask what happens if power drops for 24 hours, and how you drain the unit for service. A heater that runs constantly on a leak is an electric bill, not a water plan.

Questions to ask

  • What refill rate (gallons per minute) does the valve support?
  • How do you clean algae or sediment without shutting the pen down?
  • Is the unit rated for your lowest expected temperature?
  • Who stocks replacement valves and heating elements nearby?
