Cattle heat-stress equipment types

Agrivia editorial · August 29, 2026 · Category overview, not a herd trial

Heat stress can cut intake and performance. This page lists equipment categories commonly used in hot weather. It is not a review of products Agrivia installed on a herd and not a ranked list.

Shade

Portable shade cloth, permanent sheds, and tree lines all reduce radiant load. What works depends on stocking rate, wind, and whether cattle can bunch and still stay cool. Ask how many square feet of shade you need per head for your class of cattle, and whether the structure sheds heat or traps it.

Water

Intake rises in heat. Extra tank capacity, more linear inches of trough space, and clean, cool water matter as much as misters. A small tank that heats in the sun can limit drinking even if volume looks adequate on paper. See also our livestock waterers primer.

Air movement and wetting

Fans, sprinklers, and misters are used in confinement and some drylot setups. Over-wetting muddy lots can create other health problems. Match the method to housing, wastewater handling, and local humidity — evaporative cooling works poorly when the air is already saturated.

Monitoring

Temperature-humidity index charts, water-meter jumps, and respiration scoring help you see load before a wreck. No monitor replaces walking the pen in the afternoon.

Choose gear for your climate and housing. Confirm handling changes with your veterinarian or extension livestock specialist.
