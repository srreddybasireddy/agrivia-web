Garden water

Hose timers and drip for raised beds

By Agrivia Updated September 2026

Educational overview, not a lab ranking. We do not currently run affiliate tracking.

What to decide first

  • Clock timer: Cheap and enough if you walk the beds. It will water in the rain unless you shut it off.
  • Soil skip or rain skip: Worth it if you travel or you forget. A simple rain cup or a soil probe beats a “smart” pivot panel.
  • Drip in the bed, not a spray over the lot: Tomatoes want wet roots and dry leaves. Cattle and goats want a tank, not a mister on a hose timer.
  • Do not share one timer with a stock tank: A garden zone that drips for 20 minutes will not fill a 300-gallon cattle tank, and a tank float should not run your lettuce.

On a small place the garden and the animals both drink from the same well or hydrant, but they should not share the same clock. A hose-end timer is for beds, berries, and a few fruit trees. A tank, a float valve, or a hydrant you open by hand is for cattle, goats, sheep, horses, and pigs. Mixing those jobs is how you flood a bed or leave a herd dry.

This guide stays at backyard and homestead scale. Center pivots, VRI, and Modbus pump panels belong on a different kind of farm. Agrivia has not ranked brands in a lab. Check local frost dates and well capacity before you buy.

Clock, rain skip, soil skip

A mechanical or digital hose timer turns a faucet on for a set number of minutes. That is the whole product. If Tuesday is a storm, it still runs unless you walk out there. For six beds that is often fine — you are already feeding animals at the same hour.

Rain skip (a cup that fills, or a cheap rain sensor) stops a cycle after a measured rain. Soil skip uses a probe in one bed and holds the valve if that bed is already wet. Both fail in the usual ways: the rain cup clogged with oak catkins, the probe in the one bed that sits under a downspout, the battery dying on a Saturday. Plan to look at the plants anyway.

“Weather skip” that pulls a forecast from an app is convenient until the Wi-Fi drops or the company sunsets the hub. If you buy one, confirm there is a manual override on the faucet itself.

Drip in beds, not spray on animals

Drip tape or 1/4-inch emitters put water at the root. That is what you want for tomatoes, peppers, and a berry row. Overhead spray on a raised bed wets leaves, wastes water on paths, and still does not water cattle. If animals share a yard with beds, keep drip lines where hooves cannot snag them, or run them only inside the box.

Pressure matters. Municipal hose pressure will blow cheap tape. A $10–20 pressure reducer at the faucet is a better first purchase than a fancier timer. A well with a weak pump may not run drip and a tank fill at once — stagger them.

Livestock water is a different circuit

Cattle drink on the order of many gallons per head per day in heat; goats and sheep less; poultry much less but they still fail fast if the drinker is empty. That load belongs on a tank with a float, a hydrant, or a freeze-proof waterer — see the tanks guide. Do not put a garden drip timer on a tank fill and hope the math works.

If your only water is one frost-free hydrant, use a Y-splitter: one leg to a timer for beds (with a shutoff), one leg to a hose you open for tanks. Label both. The person who feeds at dark should not have to guess which valve is which.

Frost, batteries, and power cuts

A hose-end timer left on a faucet through a freeze can split. Drain it or bring it in. Battery units die in heat on a south wall; keep spares. If you use a 24V indoor controller, a power cut stops irrigation but does not stop a herd from needing water — tanks should not depend on the garden clock.

After a freeze, check emitters before you trust the first cycle. Ice in a line will blow fittings into a bed and look like a leak you cannot find.

Buying checklist

  • Manual override without an app.
  • Filter and pressure reducer ahead of drip.
  • Separate livestock fill from garden zones.
  • Replaceable batteries or a clear “low battery” behavior — some timers fail open (flood) and some fail closed (dry).
  • Know which failure mode you have before you leave for a weekend.

Questions people ask

Can I water pasture with the same drip timer?

A small sacrifice paddock or a tree line, maybe, with soaker hose you inspect. A cattle pasture needs rainfall, a tank, and management — not 1/4-inch emitters.

Is a “smart” controller worth it for six beds?

Often no. A clock plus looking at the plants is enough. Soil skip helps if you travel. App weather skip is extra moving parts.

It can talk through questions. Measure bed length, emitter spacing, and faucet pressure yourself. This site does not sell kits.
