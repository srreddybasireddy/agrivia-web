Irrigation controllers: timer, soil, and weather

Agrivia editorial · August 29, 2026 · Criteria overview

A controller decides when water runs. The useful split is how it decides: a clock, a soil reading, a weather station, or a mix. Agrivia has not bench-tested brands on this page.

Timer (clock) control

Simple and familiar. You set days, start times, and run minutes. It does not know if last night’s rain already filled the profile. Fine for a small block you walk daily; wasteful if you forget to skip after a storm.

Soil-based control

A probe or tensiometer starts or skips a cycle when the root zone crosses a threshold. This only works if the sensor sits in a representative spot — not a leaky fitting and not the driest ridge. Pair this with the questions in our soil moisture sensor guide.

Weather-based (ET) control

These use evapotranspiration estimates from a local station or a network. They need a reasonable crop coefficient and a working rain skip. They do not replace a walk-through when a nozzle plugs or a valve sticks.

Questions to ask

  • How many stations or zones, and can you add later?
  • Does it support rain skip, frost lockout, or flow-alarm shutoff?
  • Can you run it if the phone app or cloud account is down?
  • Who services the valves and wiring in your area?

Match the controller to water source, labor, and how often you can visit the field. Confirm schedules with your own soil and crop, not a default program.
