Soil moisture sensors: what to look for

Agrivia editorial · August 29, 2026 · Educational overview, not a field trial

A soil moisture sensor reports how wet the root zone is so you can irrigate with less guesswork. This page is a buying-criteria primer. Agrivia has not ranked brands in a lab and does not run affiliate tracking on this article.

Match the sensor to the crop and soil

Shallow vegetables, orchards, and row crops do not need the same measurement depth. Ask what depths the probe reports (for example 6, 12, and 24 inches) and whether you get volumetric water content or only a 0–100 score. Sandy soils drain faster than clay; a single shallow reading can miss a dry layer below the surface.

How the data leaves the field

Common paths are LoRaWAN, cellular, Wi‑Fi, or a logger you walk out to download. Cellular is simple if you have coverage and accept a data plan. LoRaWAN can cover a large farm if you already have a gateway. Confirm who owns the data export — CSV or API — before you buy a closed dashboard.

Power and winter

Battery and solar life depend on how often the unit transmits. Ask for expected life at your reporting interval, and whether the probe stays in the ground through freeze. Some capacitance probes are pulled before winter; others are rated for it.

Questions to ask a vendor

  • What soil depths does it report, and in what units?
  • Can you export raw readings, not only a vendor score?
  • What is the expected battery or solar life at your send interval?
  • Who installs it, and how do you calibrate for your soil type?

Confirm any irrigation change with your own records and local advice. A sensor is a tool, not a prescription.
