Field practice

Photo-based crop disease checks

Agrivia editorial · August 29, 2026 · Not a diagnosis tool

Clear photos help you describe symptoms to an agronomist or extension office. This article does not claim that Agrivia has measured a yield increase, and it does not describe a store-listed in-app model.

What to photograph

Shoot both sides of affected leaves in daylight, plus a wide shot of the plant in the row. Include healthy tissue next to the damaged area when you can. Blurry, backlit, or heavily filtered photos hide lesion color and margin — the details a specialist uses.

What to write with the photos

  • Crop, variety if known, and growth stage
  • When you first noticed it, and how fast it spread
  • Recent weather: rain, humidity, irrigation, or heat
  • Where it sits in the field (low spot, pivot edge, new seed)

How to use the pictures

Compare with extension fact sheets for your state, then confirm any spray or cultural change locally. Phone apps and chat answers can suggest look-alikes. They are not a lab assay and they do not replace a tissue test when the decision is expensive.

If you use the Agrivia website advisor, treat the reply as guidance and send the same photos to a person who knows your region.
